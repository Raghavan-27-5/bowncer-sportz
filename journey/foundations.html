Foundations

1984
1984 Station · 01 — Departure

A boy arrives in a cricket city

October 26, 1984 — Madurai, Tamil Nadu. A boy is born in a city where the game travels the streets before it ever reaches a stadium. Nobody files it as the start of anything yet. It just is.

Three decades later, the same city's earliest lessons — patience, repetition, ownership of a craft — still show up in how the academy runs its nets.

Monogram · A date set in the middle of a story
90s
'90s Station · 02

Girls' dormitory, on the off-side

At school cricket practice, the girls' dormitory sat on the off side of the ground. Hit a ball there and it carried toward the group — and drew screams from beyond the wall. The room, so the story goes, sharpened his off-side strokeplay from a mishap into a strength.

What an accidental coaching cue does to an aspiring bat: the target he was told to avoid became the one he practiced most.

This is the thing about early ground — a coach never knows which wall will teach the fighter the shot.
Field logic — off-side driving the aim
2000
2000 Station · 03

The train he told no one about

To reach district selections in Avadi, he needed permission neither his school nor his family would grant — so he engineered it: his family was told the school was taking him; the school was told he was going home. Then a ticket appeared in his hand alone.

The train left. The selections happened. He got selected — a name out of many, with nothing to recommend him but the nerve of the journey itself.

Everyone remembers the innings. Almost no one remembers the ticket.
Selected Avadi
A one-way ticket — with a secret compartment

2001 Station · 04

The city that tests

His school refused to back him further, so he moved to Chennai — a city that grants a cricketer no free passes, only a very long queue.

Early career Station · 05

Runs first, then a game

In his zonal cycle, selection didn't begin with batting averages. It began with running speed and how far he could hit a tennis ball — the two currencies an unfunded state could actually measure.

He started as a fielder. It was the long end of the craft, and it taught the craft anyway: ground fielding, quick feet, and the habit of running to every ball as if selection depended on the sprint.

Fielder first Speed + distance
The fielder's currency
2006
2006 Station · 06

A demotion, chosen on purpose

He did the thing that read like a crime to a headline writer: he dropped himself from the first division to the second, by choice, to give his whole attention to a format that India was only beginning to sell.

The format: T20.

Cricket's longest formats pay their taxes in months. T20 pays in decisions.
One rung down — the whole track ahead
T20
2006–2011 Station · 07 — The turn

One full year. One format. No list.

For an entire year he practiced only T20 — a deliberate, all-in routine with a strict diet plan ordered around a single strand of improvement. No First-Class safety. No List A comfort. Just the shortest format, repeated to mastery.

That single-minded year is why the phone call, when it finally came, didn't sound like luck.

365 days Strict diet T20 only
A target formed by focus
2011
2011 Station · 08

Two clubs noticed the all-rounder

For Fine Star he turned out in the TNCA Thiruvallur District Cricket Association First Division. For Sri Ramakrishna Mills Sports Club (SRMSC) — same season, rival range. Total of all-round performances that, between the two benches, began to be discussed beyond the district — at the level of IPL franchises.

And then — a phone call

After the year of one format and the season of those two clubs, the circuit that had been reading his numbers reached out: a trial call from Royal Challengers Bangalore. The foundations were laid. What comes next is the ring.
